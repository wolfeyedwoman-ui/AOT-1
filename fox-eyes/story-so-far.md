# Story So Far

*Last updated: end of Chapter 6 opening — the dawn ride has just left the gate.*

## Synopsis

**Evening, late autumn, year 849.** Sergeant Súile Sionnach's reconnaissance detail returns to the old Scout Regiment headquarters an hour late, through a lightning storm, with two civilians in tow: Wallis, a tenant farmer with a broken wagon wheel, and his six-year-old daughter Mina. Captain Levi — who had been waiting in the entrance hall with a mop for an excuse — receives the mud, the lateness, and the strangers on his grounds with characteristic warmth, then quietly orders his squad to feed the family, warm them, and fetch their wagon at first light.

Mina asks, at devastating volume, whether "the little grumpy one" is Súile's husband. She also rules that Levi is "little and quiet like the cat that lives in our barn." Petra defends the Captain's honor with more feeling than the question strictly required.

The real report comes after: Súile's detail went off-route before the storm because she found fresh heavy wagon ruts leaving the west road onto a disused logging track. Following them alone, on foot, downwind of a guard dog, she found a timber barn receiving crates off night wagons — crates with **military supply stamps**, sized and fitted for maneuvering gear, handled by four men who moved like soldiers and dressed like they weren't. Levi, who bought exactly that kind of merchandise from the other end of the pipeline in his Underground years, concludes the barn was *chosen* by someone with access to route maps and patrol schedules — a uniform somewhere up the chain, very possibly Military Police. The find goes to Commander Erwin by sealed letter and to no one else, not even the squad.

That night, Levi crosses the keep to Súile's tower room — a forgotten library-study-and-apothecary lined with contraband pre-Wall folklore, all of which she has secretly read — bringing tea, Petra's honey dish, and salve for the shoulder injury she left out of her report. She tells him her callsign was her mother's nickname, in a language older than any of the banned books: foxes are trickster spirits who humble the proud. He tells her — a sliver, quickly shut away — about an inheritance of one first name and a way of sitting. The honey dish stays in the tower.

**Fourth bell, the next morning.** Levi and Súile ride out disguised as two officers exercising horses, bound for the fork and the ridge — close enough to age the ruts and count the traffic, no closer. Súile has the lead from the creek onward. It's her ground.

## Open threads

- **The barn.** Recon at the ridge is happening now. Ruts to age, traffic to count, bearings to confirm. No contact.
- **The sealed letter.** En route to Erwin in Trost by courier, addressed to bypass adjutants. Erwin "collects exactly this kind of ugly" — and owns four illegal books himself.
- **The Levi ledger ("the list").** Two pages and a heading. Page one: the eye roll, "mostly," the hips remark in front of a child, the skipped honey. Page two, item one: going in alone, in lightning, within rifle range. The list keeps until the barn is dealt with. He alphabetizes.
- **Petra.** Devoted to the Captain, politely glacial to the Sergeant, counting teacups. She measured Súile's height "last time," which implies a standings table exists somewhere.
- **Wallis and Mina.** Due to be back on the road by noon with their mended wagon. Mina knows a fox lady and a barn cat when she sees them.
- **The contraband library.** Levi now knows Súile has read all of it. He has decided to be the sort of man who keeps that secret.
- **The salve tin.** Hers now. So is the honey dish.
