# Chapter 2 — Honey and Strays

Súile had barely pulled out the chair when there was a knock — two soft, perfect raps — and Petra let herself in carrying a small tray. A dish of honey. Two clean spoons. A folded cloth.

"Captain, I thought you might want—" Her eyes found the two cups already poured, and something flickered across her face, quick as a fish under ice. She recovered beautifully. "Oh. You've already made it. I brought honey — you mentioned last week the black tea's been bitter since the new supply shipment."

"Set it down."

She did, arranging the dish at Levi's elbow with the precision of a maneuver drill, and managed to place herself neatly between Levi and Súile's chair while doing it. "I also pressed your spare cravat, sir, it's in your quarters. And the training rotation for tomorrow is drafted — I can go over it with you tonight, whenever you're free." Her smile at Levi was sunlight. Then it rotated to Súile and dropped about fifteen degrees, staying flawlessly polite the entire way down. "Sergeant. You must be *exhausted*, getting so lost out there. There's stew left, if the kitchen isn't too far for you to find."

"Petra." Levi didn't look up from his tea. "The rotation can wait until morning. Out. The sergeant's reporting."

"Of course, Captain." A crisp salute, a last glance at the two cups — counting them again, as if the number might have improved — and the door clicked shut behind her with perfect gentleness.

Levi took a slow sip, palm around the rim of the cup, utterly unbothered, as though none of that had happened. Only the faint exhale through his nose suggested he was cataloguing it somewhere in that ledger of a mind.

"Petra pours the tea when I let her," he said flatly, "which is when it needs to taste like an apology." He set the cup down and slid the honey across to Súile. His eyes came up, grey and level. "Now. Seventeen hundred. You're at eighteen-oh-six. Report, Fox Eyes."

"Thank you, Petra! The honey was thoughtful!" Súile called over her shoulder. She added the honey to her own tea, stirred, sipped it. "Road conditions were abysmal. We were intercepted by a farmer with a broken wagon wheel. His child was in the back of the covered wagon, terrified of the storm. Lightning and sheets of rain, branches down everywhere. We brought them here to wait out the storm. They're in the stables now, settling their two horses."

"Oh, don't encourage her." Levi's eyes tracked the over-the-shoulder thank-you with the weary expression of a man watching two cats decide whether to fight. "She'll bring the whole hive next time."

He listened to the report without interrupting, cup at his lips, and set it down with a small, precise click when she finished.

"Let me make sure I've got this. There are two strangers and two strange horses inside a military installation, they've been on my grounds for the last ten minutes — and you let me stand here steeping tea while you took your boots off." His voice stayed level, which from Levi was the shout. "You send a rider ahead, Sergeant. One of your detail gallops in, says 'civilians inbound,' and I don't find out about it in the middle of a tea service. That's not Garrison protocol, that's not Scout protocol, that's not even common sense. Pixis teach you to collect strays, or is that your own personal defect?"

He was already up and moving as he said it, pulling his jacket off the hook — which rather undercut the lecture. He yanked the office door open on a corridor where, judging by the speed with which the mess hall went quiet, at least three members of the Special Operations Squad had been listening.

"Since you're all so free. Petra — kitchen. The stew, bread, something hot for a kid scared of thunder. Take it to the small room off the guardhouse, and get a fire going; they're not sleeping in the stables, we mucked them yesterday and I won't have it undone." If he noticed Petra's glance flick between him and Súile — measuring, again — he gave no sign of it. "Eld, log two civilians and two horses in the night book. Gunther, Oluo, there's a wagon somewhere on the west road with a broken wheel. First light, take the cart and tools and bring it in. Oluo — if you lecture the farmer about titans, I'll hear about it."

Orders dispatched, he looked back at Súile. Something in his face had settled — the storm had passed, and what was left underneath was quieter and harder to name.

"The wheel could've waited for a patrol. The kid couldn't. Fine." That was as close to *well done* as the Survey Corps was likely to hear tonight. He held the door for her, which from Levi was practically a decoration for valor. "You're coming with me to look these people over — they're your strays, you vouch for them. And afterward you still owe me the actual reconnaissance, so don't get comfortable. Move, Fox Eyes."
