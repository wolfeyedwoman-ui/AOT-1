# Chapter 7 — Pressed Cuffs

*(in progress)*

The courtyard was busy with the ordinary day — Gunther and Oluo wheeling Wallis's mended wagon toward the stables, Mina orbiting it and narrating, Eld at the night book. And on the steps of the main hall, apart from all of it, stood a man neither of them had met and both of them had memorized: sandy hair with a cowlick dropping his bangs over his forehead, a uniform pressed sharp enough to file paper, all angles and hard lines under it. He was watching the gate. He'd *been* watching the gate — and as they rode in, his fingers moved to his cuff and worked it, slow, the gesture of a man pricing something at a market stall.

Then the bangs got flicked back — theatrical, timed, a curtain going up — and Voss Grimes came down the steps to meet them with a smile that had charmed its way through fifteen years of interior garrisons, grey-blue eyes doing the arithmetic between the captain, the sergeant, the game bag, and the mud.

"Captain Ackerman. An honor, sir — Voss Grimes, transferred in yesterday." The voice was warm gravel, pitched for the whole courtyard, and then it found Súile and dropped, just for her, half a register. "And *this* would be the sergeant the west rotation can't stop talking about. Been hoping to introduce myself since I arrived." The smile widened by a calibrated centimeter. "Good hunting, was it?"

She nodded, and tossed him the bag.

The nod was courteous. The toss was *underhand* — friendly, even — and it put three head of fresh-killed game, mud, blood, feathers and all, on a flat trajectory toward the most immaculately pressed uniform inside three Walls.

He caught it. One hand, no fumble, reflexes that had nothing to do with garrison desk work — the bag smacked into his palm and his fingers closed around the neck of it like a snare going off, and that was information too. But the catch cost him: the bag swung, and a rabbit's dripping hind leg painted a dark smear across one pristine white cuff.

And there it was. A quarter of a second, no more — Súile was watching for it the way she watched a treeline, and she got it: the smile didn't fall, it *froze*, the warm gravel stopped moving through the grey-blue eyes, and something looked out of them that did arithmetic of an entirely different kind. Not annoyance. Not embarrassment. A cold, filing hatred, instant and total and *bottomless*, the kind that opens a ledger of its own and writes a debt in it with the date. Then the machinery caught, the charm rebooted, and Voss Grimes threw back his head and laughed for the whole courtyard — big, generous, a laugh you could warm your hands on — and flicked the bangs off his forehead like a man delighted by the world.

"Ha! Straight to work — I *like* that." He held the bag up, appraising it, and his eyes did one slow pass over her: the mud, the bow, the yellow eyes, the whole inventory, priced. "Rabbit and a wood pigeon. Clean kills, too." A hair's more weight on *clean* than the sentence needed. "The west rotation undersold you, Sergeant."

"FOX LADY!" Mina's voice arrived across the courtyard at full artillery elevation, from atop the mended wagon where Wallis was trying and failing to make her sit down. "DID YOU SHOOT ALL OF THOSE? PAPA, THE FOX LADY SHOOTS THINGS!" — which was, as it happened, the cover story achieving total saturation of the local information environment at zero cost. Grimes's gaze ticked toward the child, filed *fox lady* somewhere behind the grey-blue, and came back.

"Grimes." Levi had dismounted without hurrying and now existed, in that way he had, about a meter closer than anyone remembered him being. He looked terrible-tempered and slightly damp: the decoy mood, worn like the brown coat. "You're holding the squad's dinner. Kitchen's through the arch. Give it to Petra Ral — tell her it's the sergeant's kill, she'll want the count for the book." A beat, flat as a shelf. "Then find Eld Jinn for your rotation assignment. This regiment doesn't keep decorative soldiers. The uniform will recover."

"Sir." And this was its own small read: Grimes came to attention *beautifully* — parade-crisp, deferent, exactly correct — and the correctness sat on him like the civilian coat had sat on the man with the signet ring. He gathered the game bag, gave Súile a last half-register smile — "Sergeant. I'll look forward to the next introduction; I'll bring something *I* caught" — and went up the steps with the blood drying on his cuff, and never looked at it once.

Which was the loudest thing he'd done yet. A vain man checks the stain. An angry man hides that he wants to.

Levi watched him go, then took Mandrake's rein and walked with Súile toward the stables, two tired hunters and their horses, nothing in the world to discuss. The aisle swallowed the courtyard noise. He pulled the saddle, set it on the rack, and spoke to the mare's flank in the private register, barely moving air.

"Nice toss. Petra gets dinner and a spy delivers it — the squad eats and I didn't have to write a single order." A pause, buckle, strap. "Now. I saw the catch and the laugh. You saw more, because that's what you're for." The grey eyes came over the mare's back, level with hers, waiting the way he waited on the ridge.

"What did the bag buy, Fox Eyes?"
