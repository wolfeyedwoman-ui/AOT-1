# Chapter 9 — The Morning Ledger

*(in progress)*

Morning arrived the way it always did at the old keep — grey through the west window, rooks arguing on the battlements, the smell of banked embers and cold herbs.

Levi was, of course, already awake. Had been for hours; the man slept like a rumor — two hours in a chair and called it a night. He'd claimed the study chair at some point before dawn, boots on, cravat re-tied to the exact millimeter, and the good teapot was already breathing steam because some things in this world could be relied upon. He had the list open against his knee and was writing in it, pen scratching, upright rows of pins.

From the bed, upside down, one yellow eye cracked open just far enough to read the newest entry as he blotted it:

*"Teeth. — See: Fox. Ledger balanced."*

"You're awake," he said, without looking up, in the tone of a man noting the time. He folded the list, filed it in the breast pocket — patting it once, flatly, *daring* her — and poured the second cup, honey dosed, exact, setting it on the apothecary table at arm's reach from the bed. Then Captain Levi stood, and daylight rules came on with him like a lamp being lit: posture, jacket, the working register.

"Today, Sergeant. Three items." One finger. "The tin comes back — full noon, the mess, maximum audience. You'll be busy eating; let him make the trip to you. Every word to my desk within the hour." Two. "Courier's due through the fork by midday. If Erwin read the letters the way Erwin reads letters, there'll be an answer riding under a requisition stamp — nobody touches the pouch but me." Three — and here he paused at the door, hand on the bolt, and the register dropped one last degree for exactly one sentence, the way it did.

"The vow on page two applies today and all days. West rotation rides tomorrow at dawn — *both* of us. Whatever the woods say at night, Fox Eyes, you don't go hear it alone anymore."

The bolt slid. The door clicked. Somewhere down the spiral stair, fading, precisely spaced: not one single audible footstep — except, at the last landing, one floorboard, pressed once, on purpose.

*The signature.*
