# Chapter 1 — Mud on the Flagstones

*The old Scout Regiment headquarters — evening, late autumn, year 849.*

Rain had chased the reconnaissance detail home for the last three hours of the ride, and the courtyard was a churned mess of mud and horse tracks by the time Súile's unit clattered through the gate. Inside, the castle smelled of soap and lamp oil. The entrance hall's flagstones gleamed — scrubbed that afternoon, on their captain's orders, by four of humanity's strongest soldiers wearing kerchiefs over their hair.

Levi was still there. Sleeves rolled, cravat immaculate, a mop leaned against the wall beside him like a sentry as he inspected the corner joins by lamplight. He heard the doors open. He heard the boots — *her* boots; he knew the gait, the short quick stride of someone 152 centimeters tall and in an unreasonable hurry — hit his clean floor.

He didn't turn around at first.

"Stop." One word, flat as a blade laid on a table. "Take one more step in those boots, Sergeant, and you'll spend the night learning what the mop already knows."

Now he turned, slow, arms crossing, and looked her over with those narrow grey eyes — head to toe, cataloguing. Mud to the knee. Cloak dripping a widening dark ring on his flagstones. Curls flattened on one side by the rain and rioting on the other. He let the silence sit a moment too long to be casual.

"Tch. The Garrison sends us Pixis's girl, and she can't even find the boot rail by the door. Maybe on the Wall they let you drag half the district in with you." He jerked his chin toward the side passage — the boot rail, the wash basin, the folded rags stacked with military precision. "Boots off. Cloak on the hook, not the floor. Then you can tell me why your detail is back an hour late, and it had better be a report and not an excuse."

Down the corridor, Petra's head appeared around the mess hall doorframe, then wisely withdrew. Somewhere behind her, Oluo could be heard opining to Eld that *he* would never track mud past the threshold, followed shortly by the sound of him biting his tongue.

Levi ignored all of it. His eyes hadn't left Súile.

"Well? I'm waiting, brat."

She rolled her eyes, stepped out of her boots, and unclasped her cloak. "Better?"

"Marginally." Levi's gaze flicked from her stockinged feet to the cloak now hanging — crooked — on the hook. He crossed the hall, unhurried, and straightened it by two centimeters. "You hang a cloak like you were raised by titans."

He stopped in front of her. Close enough that she had to tip her chin up to hold his stare, which — he was well aware — she would do anyway, out of pure spite. The height difference was eight centimeters. She acted like it was the other way around.

"And I saw that eye roll, Sergeant. Do it again and I'll assume your eyes need something better to do — like reading every supply manifest in this castle out loud to me while I check the inventory." A beat. "Twice."

He turned on his heel and started down the corridor, clearly expecting her to follow. "Report. Walk and talk. You were due back at seventeen hundred; it's past eighteen. If a fox gets lost in the woods, I'd like to hear how, since finding things is supposedly the one thing Pixis taught you."

The corridor to his office was lamplit and spotless, of course. Through the mess hall door as they passed: Petra pretending very hard to be absorbed in her stew, Gunther not even pretending, Eld elbowing Oluo, who had adopted Levi's exact posture down to the crossed arms and was watching Súile with grave, borrowed disapproval.

In the office, a kettle already sat on the small stove — because Levi had known her detail was overdue, and had been standing in that entrance hall with his mop as an excuse. Not that he'd admit it under torture. He poured two cups of black tea and held his in that odd way of his, fingers gripped around the rim, and looked at her over it.

"Sit. Start with the road conditions, end with why you're late." His eyes narrowed a fraction — the Levi equivalent of a raised eyebrow. "And if the answer involves you doing something reckless alone, brat, think very carefully about how you phrase it."
