# The Fox Folklore

## The name

*Súile Sionnach* — "Fox Eyes" — was her mother's nickname for her. The language is ancient: pre-Wall, older than any contraband history book she has come across. She chose it as her callsign herself when she transferred to the Scouts. (Levi checked the Garrison records.)

## The tales

The west tower shelves hold nine stories with a fox in them — Levi stood there long enough to count. In every one the fox is, by his audit, "a liar. Clever, quick, gets into the henhouse, talks its way back out."

Her correction: foxes are **trickster spirits**. They behave the way they do *to bring the proud ones down a peg before they skull themselves on doorframes*. The trickery isn't the point; the humbling is.

## Applications on record

- **Levi's ruling:** "That's not a nickname, that's a *prophecy*. Erwin should've read your file closer — no, Erwin read it perfectly. He's collecting things that humble the proud. He owns four illegal books and one of me, and now a fox. The man's building a shelf."
- The proud currently scheduled for humbling: the smugglers in the barn (proud of a well-chosen hiding spot), whatever uniform chose it for them, and — by standing arrangement, mutually understood — one captain, whenever he needs it.
- Doorframe clearance is not a concern for either party. Short is survival engineering.
