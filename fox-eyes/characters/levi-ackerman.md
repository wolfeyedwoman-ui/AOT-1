# Levi Ackerman

**Rank:** Captain, Scout Regiment; commander of the Special Operations Squad
**Height / build:** 160 cm, 65 kg — dense muscle, "survival engineering"
**Canon profile:** Humanity's strongest soldier. Blunt to the point of insult, deadpan, dark-humored, obsessively clean. Grips his teacup by the rim with his whole palm. Raised in the Underground; recruited to the Scouts by Erwin Smith.

## As written in this story

Pre-Eren Levi: the squad is Eld, Gunther, Oluo, and Petra; the war is routine expeditions and the slow arithmetic of losses; the old castle is kept to a standard of cleanliness that four elite soldiers privately consider a fifth titan.

His registers, in descending order of public availability:

- **The mess-hall register.** Flat insults, mop-based threats, "tch." What the regiment sees.
- **The working register.** Quiet and even. Appears when wagon ruts lead off survey roads. The tea gets forgotten — and anyone who knows him knows what forgotten tea means.
- **The other register.** Lower than the rain. Heard only on the far side of the keep, where the corridor goes nowhere else and the bolt is never thrown.

## The ledger ("the list")

Levi processes Súile's insubordination by entering it into a running mental ledger, announced aloud, with a heading, allegedly alphabetized. The list is currently two pages. It "keeps until the barn's dealt with." Items are distinguished from actual discipline: going in alone within rifle range of armed smugglers is Page Two, Item One; the rest is foreplay-by-bureaucracy that both parties pretend is discipline.

His actual care surfaces sideways: a kettle already hot because a detail was overdue; both horses saddled before fourth bell; a salve tin produced from a jacket pocket for an injury she never reported, applied with "killer's hands, careful as a bookbinder's"; the good lamp, handed over without comment.

## The Underground, in slivers

He doesn't discuss it. Twice now it has surfaced anyway: he bought black-market gear "with the stamps filed off" from the other end of the same pipeline he's now hunting, and — offered once, by firelight, then shut away — his inheritance was "a first name and a way of sitting. She drank tea like it mattered."

Where he grew up, nobody had nicknames. They had aliases, earned face-down.

## Selected rulings

- On ghosts: "No ghosts. They wouldn't pass inspection."
- On height: "Short is survival engineering, Sergeant. We're not small. We're *efficient*."
- On being compared to a barn cat: received as the finest compliment of the year.
- On the Scouts, to a six-year-old: "We go outside so that one day a kid like you can stand on that Wall and be bored by the view."
